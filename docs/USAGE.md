# Using FocusFlow

Open `Start-FocusFlow.cmd` on Windows or `Start-FocusFlow.command` on macOS to build and open your everyday workspace. Personal and manual project work are available as soon as the local app opens. See [the README](../README.md) for setup.

Closing the window keeps FocusFlow running in the tray (Windows) or the menu bar (Mac). Click its icon to open the window again, and choose **Quit FocusFlow** from the icon's menu to end it. Long lists in **Upcoming**, **History** and **Points history** show 20 entries at first; choose **Show more** for older ones.

## Personal

**Today** shows what to do now. Timer cards for timed habits sit at the top, then **Habits**, then one-off and repeating **Tasks**. Use **Upcoming** for future tasks and **History** for resolved ones. Click a card's heading or an empty part of the card to fold it to its title, and click again to open it; clicking a row or its buttons never folds the card. FocusFlow remembers folded cards. **Habits completed today** and **Tasks completed today** start folded. A missed one-off task keeps its original date and stays open until you complete or cancel it.

### Add tasks and habits

Type in **Add a task for today** and press Enter for a quick task. **New task** and **More options** open the task window with every field on one page; choose **One-off** or **Repeating task**. A repeating task can repeat daily, on chosen weekdays, monthly, quarterly or yearly; habits repeat daily or weekly. A month-based task repeats on the day of month it starts, or on the last day of shorter months, and stays open until the next one is due. **New habit** walks through four short steps: what and when, the moment it follows, the two-minute version, and the reward and identity. A habit or repeating task records counts or minutes toward a small daily goal. The window follows the four habit laws from *Atomic Habits*:

- **Make it obvious:** a cue such as "After I pour my morning coffee", plus a time and a place.
- **Make it attractive:** the points it earns, something you enjoy to pair it with, and the reward you are working toward.
- **Make it easy:** a two-minute version that counts as showing up, and a small daily goal.
- **Make it satisfying:** the identity each completion votes for, such as "I am a reader". Choose a saved identity or enter your own.

Only the title is required. The summary at the bottom shows the whole plan. If you already build three habits, the New habit window shows a gentle note. One-off tasks show title, date, time, place and completion points. Repeated activity can be logged several times on its scheduled day, including beyond the goal: a goal of three stretches can show 10/3, with points for all ten. Each task counts once in its category progress when its goal is met; a habit counts once it is checked in.

### Timer cards

The cards at the top hold today's timed habits that have not reached their goal yet, such as reading or meditation. Swipe, drag or use the arrows to move between them. Planned times that have arrived come first, then habits without a time, then later times; a habit you already started today moves to the end. **Start 2 minutes** is the way in: the timer keeps counting quietly for as long as you go, nothing interrupts you at two minutes, and it keeps running while you switch views. **Stop** adds the elapsed minutes to that habit, and **Cancel timer** discards it. To record without the timer, use **Log minutes** on the habit in the list.

### Record and track

Tick the square to complete a task and use the circle to check in a habit. A timed or counted habit climbs three tiers each day: **check in** (the two-minute version, easy even on a busy day), **maintain** and **goal**. Its main button records the rest of the next tier in one tap, such as **Check in · 2 min** or **Maintain · 8 min more**; **Other amount** records any amount. Marks on its bar show the tiers. Set the check-in and maintain amounts in the editor, or leave them empty for two minutes (one count) and the point halfway to the goal. Checking in closes the habit for the day's ring, and reaching the goal earns a bonus of a fifth of the goal's points, shown as **Goal bonus** in Points history. Every habit shows its recent occurrences, how many of the latest seven you showed up for, and its chain. Any logged minutes count as showing up. One miss does not break the chain; two misses in a row do, and the habit reminds you never to miss twice. **Rest today** records a planned day off that never counts as a miss. A habit with an identity shows its votes so far: every day done or started is one vote for that identity, across all habits that name it.

Each habit you finish today gets a short, quiet moment that grows as the day fills: a fine ripple from where you tapped and a small note at the top with your progress. When every habit is done, a ring closes in the middle of the screen, with a soft chord if sound is on, and shows how many days in a row you have done them all. Click it or wait a few seconds. With reduced motion on, only the notes and the finished ring appear.

After you complete or cancel a task, choose **Undo** next to the message. **History**, **Habits completed today** and **Tasks completed today** offer **Reopen** for one-off tasks, and for a repeating occurrence while its period is current. Reopening a completed task returns the points it earned. History shows the latest 200 resolved tasks; older records stay saved.

### Focus

Choose the star on up to three one-off tasks to make them today's **Focus**. They move to their own group at the top and show how many are done; the rest stay under **Other tasks**. A task you move to another date leaves the focus.

### Close the day

From 6 PM, Today offers **Close the day**, about a minute of evening review; the panel beside Today opens it at any time. For habits with nothing logged today, **2 min now** starts the timer at the top of Today and **Rest today** records a planned rest day. One-off tasks still open can move to **Tomorrow** or another date, or be dropped; open repeating tasks can skip the current occurrence. Then pick up to three tasks as tomorrow's focus and read a tip for preparing tomorrow's first planned habit. **Done for today** or **Not tonight** hides the evening prompt until the next day.

### Weekly review

Once a habit has about a week of records, Today offers a **Weekly review** each week; the panel opens it at any time. It shows how often you showed up in the last seven days compared with the week before, the votes for each identity, and one suggestion per habit. After two steady weeks (85% or more) it suggests a slightly higher daily target for a timed habit, or stacking a new habit right after a steady one. After a week under half it suggests an easier target, a smaller start or a different cue. A habit steady for 66 days or more can **Graduate**: it leaves Today and keeps its history. More than three habits at once gets a gentle note. **Done for this week** or **Not this week** hides the prompt until next Monday.

### Edit, delete and schedules

Use the pencil on a task or habit to edit its plan. In the editor, choose the pencil beside the name to rename it; open occurrences take the new name and History keeps the old one. A repeat with nothing due today can be edited from its row in **Upcoming** or **History**. For a repeat, choose the effective date, up to four weeks ahead, and how often it repeats; a weekly plan can run on selected weekdays. A task that becomes a habit moves to a daily or weekly schedule. Until the effective date the current schedule continues, so its remaining occurrences appear in **Upcoming**. Recorded activity keeps its original dates. A check-off habit made before counts existed stays a check-off habit when you edit it. Older repeating schedules keep their habit grouping; to change one, select **Repeating task** in **Edit plan** and save.

From the editor, delete a single task, one occurrence, or the whole repeating plan. Deleting a plan removes its open occurrences and keeps past records and earned points. Daily and selected-weekday occurrences follow the local calendar; older weekly schedules keep their seven-day periods, and a missed period is recorded once. To end a repeating plan, delete it from the editor; a steady habit can also **Graduate** in the weekly review.

### Points and rewards

The panel beside Today shows a pink habit ring and a green task ring, today's points, the last seven days, your balance and progress toward a pinned reward. **Rewards** lets you add and price your own rewards; the balance carries forward and pays for redemptions. **Archive** asks for confirmation and removes a reward from the shop while keeping its history. In **History > Points history**, **Correct log** adjusts an amount or moves it to another scheduled date at the original rate, and supported entries can be undone after confirming. Open **Settings > Sound and motion** for sounds, volume and reduced motion.

## Projects

The **Action center** lists tickets waiting for review, work in progress and the next eligible Backlog ticket. Start a manual ticket to move it to **In progress**. Open the ticket to read its description and acceptance criteria. For coding tickets, use the run panel inside the ticket.

Choose **New project** in the sidebar, add a milestone and then create a ticket. Use the milestone selector and **New ticket** in either the milestone list or the status board. The board shows all milestones. A ticket uses Backlog, In progress, Review or Done. To change the title, description or acceptance criteria, open its details and choose **Edit ticket**. A failed save keeps your draft.

**Set prerequisites** links a ticket to work that needs to finish first. **Add blocker** records an external reason. Follow a blocker link to the exact ticket. A Backlog ticket becomes eligible once its prerequisites are Done and its manual blocker is cleared. Starting it remains your choice.

To remove a project, choose **Delete project** and type its name. This permanently removes its local tasks and project history. GitHub Issues and pull requests, and the worktree files, stay available.

## Optional connections

Open **AI connection** above **Settings**. Choose OpenAI, Claude or DeepSeek to enter an API key. Connecting reads the provider's model list. Pick the connection and model in a project before sending a request. For Codex, run `npm run ai:install` from the source checkout and follow **Sign in with ChatGPT**. Uses are charged to the selected ChatGPT Codex allowance. For the optional DeepSeek Harness, install the CLI shown in the dialog and save a key there, or supply the existing `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` environment setting. Compatible API connections can use a configured endpoint and key. These requests use the selected API balance. Open **Settings** to change the interface language, startup workspace or size. Save to apply the language and size immediately. Each local profile keeps its own preferences.

The desktop protects saved API keys with Windows encryption or the macOS Keychain.

Open **GitHub connection** in the project to authorize an account and link a repository. The app prompts for explicit approval before publishing. [GitHub setup and permissions](GITHUB_CONNECTION.md)

## Plan and run work

- **AI plan draft** turns a project goal into an editable proposal. Check the model and effort before generating. Review selected milestones, tickets and dependencies before approving the draft.
- **Run AI** in a ticket can break the work into smaller tickets or produce a written result. Assign every original acceptance item to a selected child before creating tickets.
- **Context and settings** holds project requirements, local repository, preview command and separate model choices. **Read repository references** shows excerpts from committed documents. Edit an excerpt and save settings to add it. This read uses no model calls.
- Use **Run coding ticket** to review the source folder, destination visibility, base commit and model before starting. If an Issue is needed, review the exact proposed Issue. If setup fails after publishing it, refresh the run details so the app can use the existing Issue. **Publish approved work** is also available for a batch of approved coding tickets.
- Codex runs in the prepared ticket worktree. Scope fixes, Stop and permission choices are in the ticket. The selected context and exact prompt are recorded locally. DeepSeek and compatible API coding drafts a patch for explicit tracked text files. Review the saved diff and choose **Apply this patch** or **Discard patch**.
- **Preview** opens the saved command from that ticket's worktree. For FocusFlow use `focusflow`, which opens a separate named review workspace. For a web project use an `npm run SCRIPT` entry that reports a loopback address. Use **Stop preview** to stop the tracked process. If the app cannot verify its identity, close the old preview manually.
- Review the actual diff and destination before publishing a PR. Refresh review evidence after the PR head, ticket requirements or project context changes. Ask for explanations or request a revision from the ticket. Merge explicitly on GitHub. FocusFlow marks the coding ticket Done after it observes the matching merge.

## Review workspaces and recovery

Run `./Start-FocusFlow.command --review my-review` on Mac or `.\Start-FocusFlow.cmd --review my-review` on Windows to open a separate workspace. New names start empty. Reopen the same name and database format to keep your changes. The window displays **Separate workspace**. Run the launcher without arguments to open everyday data.

Existing everyday data is retained across checkouts. Review and development paths are documented in [Development](DEVELOPMENT.md#data-paths). Schema upgrades take a verified backup first. See [Backup and recovery](DEVELOPMENT.md#backup-and-recovery) if you need to recover a database.
